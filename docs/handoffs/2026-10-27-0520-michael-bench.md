## What this is, plainly
A deterministic bench of rule-based students run through the mock. It measures **the mock primer's logic, not learning**: whether the arc finds each planted gap, how many probes it spends, which items it shows, and whether the fresh problem after a repair comes out right. Its value is (1) catching regressions in the demo arc and (2) the fixtures and expected outcomes, which Connor's real bench (Python, `src/platy/evaluation`, the `simulated_student` role) can reuse. **The YC learning number is still R-26 with real teens.**

## What changed
- **`web/src/bench/students.json`:** eight students, plain JSON: `no-gap`; `gap-inverse-operations`; `gap-one-step-equations`; `slip-constant-moved` (`constant_moved_without_undoing`); `slip-coefficient` (`coefficient_subtracted`, a one-step slip on line 2); `gap-inverse-with-slip`; `two-gaps` (one-step + inverse); `gap-integer-arithmetic`. Each is `{id, gaps, slips, answers_right_elsewhere, expected: {found, note?}}`.
- **`web/src/bench/student.ts`:** `SimStudent`, a rule, not a learner. A gap makes every problem on that node, and on every node that needs it (the skill map's prerequisite chains), wrong until that node is taught; a slip is one known wrong line (`2x + 5 = 17 → 2x = 22`; `2x = 12 → x = 10`) every time until its repair; everything else right. It knows the answers the way the mock does (`linearForm`, the map's probe answers); a wrong answer is the slip's value from `slipValues` when there is one, else one off.
- **`web/src/bench/run.ts`:** `runStudent(fixture)` drives a `MockTransport` on the two step-by-step problems with no waits: it answers the beat (certain), resumes a pause, taps every "next up", skips through each item (counting it) and writes lines or answers until `session_end` or the turn cap (80). It records the `gap_found` hypothesis, probes, items, repairs, teaches, every primer move, and per node whether the first lesson problem and the fresh problem were solved with no miss.
- **`web/src/bench/report.ts`** and **`npm --prefix web run bench`** (`web/scripts/bench.mjs`, Vite's module runner like the coverage script): per student planted vs found, probes, items, repairs, the fresh problem's verdict, how it ended, and the fixture's note; then per node before/after counts. Stdout only; nothing in `var/`.
- README: the structure list and a paragraph on the bench.

## Two limits of the mock the bench pins (for Connor's policy)
- **A gap below two levels is misnamed.** With an integer-arithmetic gap, both probes miss and the mock names the deepest probe, inverse operations; the explanation doesn't land, the angle switches, and the session stalls. R-01 says one to two levels; the real policy needs a rule for a miss on the deepest probe (probe once more, or teach the deepest and re-probe after).
- **The mock never probes again after teaching.** With two gaps it finds the deeper one and then stalls on the other. The real policy should re-probe when a taught skill's problem is still missed.

## Decisions and why
- **A gap fails dependents too** (not only problems on the node itself). Under the literal "wrong only on the gap node" rule the student would solve `2x + 5 = 17` and never be probed, so no gap could ever be found; prerequisite failure is what makes probing meaningful.
- **Students skip through items** rather than play them. Playing puzzles would need the puzzle's private state or its own solver; the bench counts items and plants the learning at the item's end, which is what the outcome measures need.
- **`expected.found` is explicit in every fixture**, with a note where it differs from the planted gap, so the two limits above are recorded as expected outcomes rather than hidden by a loose test.
- **The lesson is the two step problems** (`STEP_LESSON`), the arc the bench is about; the story problem (H) isn't in it, so the bench needs no setup rule.
- **"Right first time" means solved with no wrong line**, not a right first line, or the coefficient-slip student would count as right.
